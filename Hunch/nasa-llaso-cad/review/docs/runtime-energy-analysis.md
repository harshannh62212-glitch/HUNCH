# Rover Runtime, Dock Charge & Cargo-Run Analysis (5 Ah packs)

Generated by `tools/energy_runtime_sim.js` (uses the simulator's own `rover_bom.js` power model and the dock charger law in `dock_station.js`). Re-run: `node tools/energy_runtime_sim.js`.

## Assumptions
- Two identical 12.8 V × 5 Ah LiFePO4 packs = 64 Wh nameplate, **derated 20 % to 51.2 Wh** for ageing and cold: one on the rover, one spare charging in the dock. Hot-swap takes 25 s with the door closed.
- **25 % SoC floor** (12.8 Wh) is never planned into, which leaves reserve for retries. Missions start at **80 %**, so about **25.6 Wh** is usable per sortie.
- Dock charger: **64 W CC (5 A)**, 60.2 W into the pack, taper above 90 % SoC. PV bus is **4 × 70 W = 266 W** at the bus (code: `dock_station.js` `PV_BUS_W`); rover has priority and the spare gets the leftover, which is plenty, so both charge at full rate in daylight.
- One cargo run = pick up pod, carry it to the habitat, place it. Dock sits beside the habitat (20 m legs). Pod mass ratio 1.25, terrain factor 1.15 on drive power. A **3 min dock cycle** (approach, door, latch, depart) is added to every sortie.
- Daylight only. At night the 1280 Wh (100 Ah LiFePO4) station buffer only runs survival heaters; no charging. LiFePO4 cannot charge below 0 °C, so the code blocks charging when the warm box is below 0 °C.

## Charge times (dock, daylight)
| Case | Time |
|---|---|
| Pack 25 % → 80 % (ready to depart) | **about 28 min** |
| Pack 25 % → 100 % | **about 45 min** |
| Empty 0 % → 80 % / 100 % | 41 / 58 min |

With 266 W of PV the spare charges at the same time as the rover, so the charger, not the PV, sets the pace.

## Cargo runs per charge (135 m lander–habitat sim route)
| Power mode | Speed | Wh per delivery | Runs from 80 % | Runs from 100 % | Sortie length (80 %) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Range | 0.48 m/s | 7.55 | 3 | 4 | 37 min |
| **Eco (default)** | 0.62 m/s | 6.85 | **3** | 5 | 30 min |
| Balance | 0.78 m/s | 6.01 | 4 | 6 | 33 min |
| Boost | 1.00 m/s | 5.21 | 5 | 7 | 34 min |

Faster is more energy-efficient per delivery because avionics/lidar/Pi baseline (~13 W) dominates over drive power. The `whPerRoundTrip` values in `rover_bom.js` now come from this table.

## Sustained 8 h of daylight (135 m route)
| Mode | Single pack (wait to recharge) | Two packs (swap) |
|---|---|---|
| Eco | 30 deliveries (3.8/h), 3.3 h waiting | **53 deliveries (6.6/h)**, 10 swaps, 0.0 h waiting |
| Boost | 40 deliveries (5.0/h), 3.5 h waiting | **75 deliveries (9.4/h)**, 10 swaps, 0.0 h waiting |

The swap pack nearly doubles throughput. Charge time (28–45 min) is close to a sortie (30–34 min), so a swap pack removes nearly all waiting. The margin is thin, so the dock cycle overhead and the 25 % floor matter.

## Finding: the full 2 km route does not fit a 5 Ah pack
A single 2 km delivery (out, carry, return) needs 67–104 Wh against about 25.6 Wh usable from 80 %. Options: a mid-route charging post, 20 Ah packs (previous BOM), or scale the demo course (135 m) as the test route.

## Night
The 1280 Wh buffer runs the 2.1 W survival load for about 488 h, which covers a 354 h night with margin. Rover charging is off at night. Plan cargo runs in daylight. The design note (`dock-station-design.md`) was written for a 5 Ah LTO buffer and has been updated to match the code.

## What changed in the simulator
- Rover pack 5 Ah / 64 Wh (`rover_bom.js`), starts at 80 %.
- Spare pack in the dock (`NASA_Dock.state().spareWh`), charged from leftover PV; auto swap when docked if the spare is ≥15 % better (25 s).
- Low-battery supervisor: automatic return-to-dock when SoC nears floor + trip cost.
- HUD (bottom-left): rover %, spare % + charge W, swaps, sorties, average Wh per sortie, runs left.
- Sortie log lines: `[SORTIE #n] x Wh in y min`, `[PACK SWAP #n]`, `[POWER] ready for next sortie`.
