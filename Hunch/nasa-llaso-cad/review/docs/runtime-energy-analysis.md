# Rover Runtime, Dock Charge & Cargo-Run Analysis (5 Ah packs)

Generated by `tools/energy_runtime_sim.js` (uses the simulator's own `rover_bom.js` power model and the dock charger law in `dock_station.js`). Re-run: `node tools/energy_runtime_sim.js`.

## Assumptions
- Two identical 12.8 V × 5 Ah LiFePO4 packs = **64 Wh each**: one on the rover, one spare charging in the dock. Hot-swap takes 25 s with the door closed.
- 18 % SoC floor (11.5 Wh) is never used. Missions start at **80 % (51.2 Wh)**, so **39.7 Wh** is usable per sortie.
- Dock charger: 41 W CC (38.5 W into the pack), taper above 90 % SoC. PV bus 66.5 W minus 5 W standby; rover has priority, the spare gets the leftover.
- One cargo run = pick up pod, carry it to the habitat, place it. Dock sits beside the habitat (20 m legs). Pod mass ratio 1.25, terrain factor 1.15 on drive power (calibrated: marginal 6.85 Wh per 135 m round trip vs. the BOM's 6.9 Wh).
- Daylight only. At night the 69 Wh station buffer only runs survival heaters; no charging.

## Charge times (dock, daylight)
| Case | Time |
|---|---|
| Pack 18 % → 80 % (ready to depart) | **62 min** |
| Pack 18 % → 100 % | **96 min** |
| Empty 0 % → 80 % / 100 % | 80 / 114 min |

While the rover is docked and charging at 41 W, the spare gets only ~16 W of leftover PV; with the rover out, the spare charges at the full 41 W.

## Cargo runs per charge (135 m lander–habitat sim route)
| Power mode | Speed | Runs from 80 % | Runs from 100 % | Sortie length (80 %) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Range | 0.48 m/s | 5 | 6 | 56 min |
| **Eco (default)** | 0.62 m/s | **5** | 7 | 45 min |
| Balance | 0.78 m/s | 6 | 8 | 44 min |
| Boost | 1.00 m/s | 7 | 9 | 43 min |

Faster is more energy-efficient because avionics/lidar/Pi baseline (~13 W) dominates over drive power.

## Sustained 8 h of daylight (135 m route)
| Mode | Single pack (wait to recharge) | Two packs (swap) |
|---|---|---|
| Eco | 25 deliveries (3.1/h), 4.3 h idle | **46 deliveries (5.8/h)**, 6 swaps, 1.1 h idle |
| Boost | 35 deliveries (4.4/h) | **55 deliveries (6.9/h)**, 3 swaps |

The swap pack nearly doubles throughput. Charge time (62–96 min) is longer than a sortie (45 min), so the dock charger, not the rover, is the bottleneck; a third pack or a second charger would remove the remaining wait.

## Finding: the full 2 km route does not fit a 5 Ah pack
One-way loaded cost for 2 km is 36 Wh (boost) to 56 Wh (range) against 39.7 Wh usable from 80 %. A single 2 km delivery (out, carry, return) needs 67–104 Wh. Options: a mid-route charging post, 20 Ah packs (previous BOM), or scale the demo course (135 m) as the NASA brief allows for the prototype.

## Night
Station buffer (69 Wh) holds survival heaters ~26 h of a 354 h night; no rover charging at night. Plan cargo runs in daylight.

## What changed in the simulator
- Rover pack 5 Ah / 64 Wh (`rover_bom.js`), starts at 80 %.
- Spare pack in the dock (`NASA_Dock.state().spareWh`), charged from leftover PV; auto swap when docked if the spare is ≥15 % better (25 s).
- Low-battery supervisor: automatic return-to-dock when SoC nears floor + trip cost.
- HUD (bottom-left): rover %, spare % + charge W, swaps, sorties, average Wh per sortie, runs left.
- Sortie log lines: `[SORTIE #n] x Wh in y min`, `[PACK SWAP #n]`, `[POWER] ready for next sortie`.
